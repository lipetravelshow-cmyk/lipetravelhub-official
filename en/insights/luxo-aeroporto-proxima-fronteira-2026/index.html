The Next Frontier of Luxury May Begin Before the Aircraft Door Closes

Private terminals, dedicated security, concierge service and airside transfers are turning the airport from a necessary passage into part of the premium journey. Luxury increasingly means removing friction before takeoff.

Lipe CamanzanoPublished Sep 5, 20268 min read
LTH reading · September 2026. Airport luxury is moving beyond the lounge toward friction removal. When passengers buy time, predictability and privacy, the airport stops being only infrastructure and begins to operate as a product.

For years, the premium airport experience was largely summarized by lounges. That is changing. In Dallas and Miami, PS allows commercial-airline passengers to use private terminals, go through dedicated security processes and be driven airside to the aircraft. In São Paulo, Terminal BTG Pactual at Guarulhos offers a similar proposition for commercial travelers.

The product is not the room. It is the absence of friction.

The real value lies in removing unpleasant or unpredictable steps from the journey: queues, long walks, exposure, waiting, baggage handling and lack of control over time.

At PS, the experience may include a separate entrance, dedicated screening, private areas and airside transfer. At GRU, Terminal BTG Pactual offers private access, check-in, baggage drop, X-ray, immigration and customs in dedicated areas, plus transport to the aircraft.

Luxury travel is becoming journey design

Premium travelers increasingly see hotel, airport, transfer and flight as one continuous experience. An excellent hotel followed by a chaotic departure reduces the perceived value of the whole journey. That creates room for tighter integration among hotels, airports, airlines, cards and banks, private aviation, ground transportation and concierge services.

The new luxury is not adding more things to the trip. It is removing what wastes time and attention.

The airport enters the experience economy

Architecture, food, wellness, privacy and hospitality are already part of the best lounges. Private terminals take the idea further by redesigning the process, not just the environment. They also create new revenue categories, bringing services historically associated with business aviation to high-value commercial passengers without requiring them to charter a private jet.

Who owns the customer?

There is a quiet customer-ownership contest. If a bank, private terminal or concierge coordinates arrival, baggage, security, lounge and ground movement, it controls an increasing share of the relationship — and potentially the data — before boarding even begins.

What this means for hospitality and distribution

For luxury hotels, travel advisors, premium TMCs and platforms, the airport becomes part of the product. The opportunity is continuity: room to aircraft, aircraft to resort, fewer handoffs and more confidence.
