Travel AI Has Entered the Agentic Phase

AI is moving from itinerary suggestions to action: comparing, monitoring and increasingly participating in bookings. That shift is reshaping distribution, trust and who owns the customer relationship.

Lipe CamanzanoPublished Sep 4, 202610 min read
Editorial update · September 2026. This analysis revisits an earlier Lipe Travel Hub reflection on AI in travel, incorporating the latest moves from Google, Expedia Group and Booking.com, together with the shift toward AI agents.

The most important change is not conversation. It is action.

The first wave of AI in travel looked like a smarter interface for familiar tasks: search, destination inspiration, itinerary building and customer questions. In 2026, that stage began to give way to something more consequential. Agentic systems do not simply recommend; they monitor prices, combine preferences, complete steps in the journey and increasingly participate in transactions.

Google moved in that direction by bringing price tracking and hotel booking into AI Mode in Search. Expedia Group expanded AI agents and tools for travelers and partners and acquired Layla, a conversational trip-planning platform. The question is no longer whether AI will enter travel. It is who controls intent, distribution and the customer relationship when AI begins to act on the traveler’s behalf.

The new intermediary may be invisible

Travel intermediaries used to be easy to identify: agency, tour operator, OTA, metasearch engine, loyalty program. AI agents can sit before all of them. A traveler describes intent, budget, constraints and preferences; the agent interprets them and searches across multiple sources.

That makes a less visible layer strategically important: data quality, accessible inventory, APIs, structured content, reputation, commercial rules and the ability to serve external systems. Distribution increasingly depends on being legible to machines as well as persuasive to people.

Discovery, comparison and purchase are moving closer together

Booking inside search experiences points to a compressed journey. Inspiration, research, comparison, click and purchase no longer need to happen in separate environments. The shorter the distance between intent and transaction, the greater the competition for the first recommendation — and for the relationship after the sale.

SEO remains important, but hotels, destinations, OTAs and suppliers will also need to think about presence inside answers, agents and decision flows that may never generate a conventional site visit.

The paradox: more automation, more need for trust

2026 data shows travelers increasingly using AI for inspiration and planning while remaining cautious about delegating purchases. Booking.com research in Brazil found high adoption of AI for travel planning but significantly lower confidence in using it to buy flights. Expedia Group found a similar pattern internationally: travelers experiment with AI but still prefer established travel brands to book and solve problems.

The agent does not eliminate the brand. It increases the value of brands that can provide operational certainty, transparency and support when the trip deviates from plan.

Competition now includes the agent’s trust

Travel companies also need to become trustworthy to AI systems themselves. An agent is more likely to rely on sources with consistent inventory, structured information, verifiable availability, clear policies and strong reputation. The question therefore expands from “how do I persuade the customer?” to “how do I make my product understandable, eligible and reliable for the systems helping that customer decide?”

Value shifts toward integration, context and exception handling

Agents are highly efficient when tasks are repeatable and information is organized. Travel, however, is full of exceptions: missed connections, weather disruption, overbooking, documentation, corporate policy, family complexity and situations in which a wrong decision is expensive. Infrastructure, service and journey design therefore return to the center.

The best agentic experience will not automate everything. It will know when to act, when to confirm, when to ask for authorization and when to transfer the case to a person without losing context.

2026 is the year AI became travel distribution infrastructure

The moves by Google, Expedia Group and other platforms suggest AI is leaving the edge of the journey and moving into its infrastructure. For companies and destinations, the practical agenda includes structured data, APIs, agent-ready content, governance, observability, service design and customer-ownership strategy.

The AI race in travel will not be won by the best interface alone. It will be won by those able to connect intelligence, inventory, trust and execution — and remain relevant even when part of the journey happens outside their own channels.
